\begin{abstract}
This report presents a validation of the Supervised Learning -- Global Pathway Selection (SL-GPS) method for adaptive chemical mechanism reduction applied to methyl formate (CH$_3$OCHO) combustion using the ThInK~1.0 mechanism (160 species, 1089 reactions). The complete pipeline is documented: training data generation via GPS, neural network training, and validation against the detailed mechanism across 8 test cases spanning temperatures of 800--1500~K, pressures of 1--20~atm, and equivalence ratios of 0.7--1.3. All training parameters, species classifications, and results are reported for full reproducibility.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

The SL-GPS method trains neural networks on Global Pathway Selection (GPS) data to dynamically predict which chemical species are important during combustion simulations. At each timestep, the ANN evaluates the current thermochemical state and activates only the predicted species, producing an adaptive reduced mechanism that varies in size throughout the simulation.

This report validates SL-GPS on the ThInK~1.0 mechanism \cite{think1} for methyl formate (CH$_3$OCHO, also known as methyl methanoate), a simple oxygenated fuel of interest as a biodiesel surrogate and a key intermediate in dimethyl ether (DME) and dimethoxymethane (DMM) oxidation.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{SL-GPS Pipeline}

The pipeline consists of three stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data generation:} Run $N$ autoignition simulations with randomized initial conditions ($T$, $P$, $\phi$). At each timestep interval, apply GPS ($\alpha = 0.001$) to label which species are important.
    \item \textbf{ANN training:} Train an ensemble of neural networks on the GPS-labeled data. Select the best model by validation loss.
    \item \textbf{Validation:} Run new autoignition cases with the detailed mechanism and simultaneously with SL-GPS (ANN-guided reduction). Compare temperature, HRR, species profiles, and ignition delay.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Species Classification}

Before training, species are partitioned into three groups based on occurrence frequency in the GPS training data:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Always} ($>99\%$ occurrence): unconditionally included in all reduced mechanisms
    \item \textbf{Variable} (1--99\%): predicted by the ANN at each timestep
    \item \textbf{Never} ($<1\%$ occurrence): permanently excluded
\end{itemize}
Only the \emph{variable} species are outputs of the neural network.

\subsection{Neural Network Architecture}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input:} temperature, pressure, and 14 selected species mole fractions (normalized via MinMaxScaler)
    \item \textbf{Hidden layers:} 2 fully-connected layers $\times$ 32 neurons with ReLU activation
    \item \textbf{Output:} sigmoid activation (one output per variable species)
    \item \textbf{Loss:} binary cross-entropy; \textbf{Optimizer:} Adam
    \item \textbf{Training:} ensemble of 8 models in parallel, best selected by validation loss
    \item \textbf{Early stopping:} patience = 30 epochs, max = 200 epochs
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Accuracy}

Across 7 of 8 validation cases, SL-GPS reproduces ignition delay times with a mean error of 2.4\%, demonstrating that the ANN accurately captures the GPS species selection for methyl formate combustion. Temperature profiles, heat release rates, and major species evolution closely match the detailed mechanism.

\subsection{Failed Case Analysis}

The T900\_P10 case ($T_0=900$~K, $P=10$~atm) exhibits a 165\% ignition delay error. This condition falls in the low-temperature / intermediate-pressure regime where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Methyl formate undergoes complex low-temperature oxidation pathways
    \item The competition between thermal decomposition and H-abstraction channels is sensitive to radical pool composition
    \item The ANN may lack sufficient training data at this specific condition
\end{itemize}
This failure was subsequently root-caused and resolved; see Section~\ref{sec:revision2}.

\subsection{Mechanism Reduction}

The adaptive SL-GPS approach reduces the ThInK~1.0 mechanism from 160 species to an average of 61 active species (62\% reduction). The species count varies dynamically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-ignition:} $\sim$55--60 species active
    \item \textbf{During ignition:} peak at $\sim$65 species as radical pool builds
    \item \textbf{Post-ignition:} relaxation to $\sim$58 species
\end{itemize}

\section{Revision 2: NTC Investigation and Pipeline Hardening}
\label{sec:revision2}
% =============================================================================

Following the baseline study, the T900\_P10 failure was root-caused through a
controlled diagnostic campaign. Classic GPS applied to the failing trajectories
reproduced ignition correctly with as few as 22 species, proving the reduction
method itself sound and isolating all errors to the supervised-learning layer.
Five distinct defects were identified and fixed:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Frozen-state ANN input (code bug).} The ANN input composition
    was never updated during the adaptive simulation: only temperature evolved
    while species mole fractions remained at their initial values. In two-stage
    (NTC) ignition, composition carries the dominant state information, so this
    was catastrophic at low temperature (T900\_P10: 165\%). Fixed in
    \texttt{slgps/utils.py} (\texttt{auto\_ign\_build\_SL}); gated by the
    environment variable \texttt{SLGPS\_FROZEN\_STATE\_FIX} (default on).

    \item \textbf{Never-list frequency threshold.} Species selected by GPS in
    fewer than 1\% of training samples were permanently excluded from all
    reduced mechanisms. Under regime-unbalanced training data this silently
    blocked rare-but-critical species (e.g.\ CH at high temperature). Revised
    rule: only species GPS \emph{never} selects (frequency $=0$) are excluded.

    \item \textbf{Always-list erosion under rebalancing.} Merging datasets
    diluted H$_2$ occurrence just below the 99\% always-threshold, demoting it
    to ANN control and collapsing low-temperature ignition (H$_2$/HO$_2$/H$_2$O$_2$
    chain). Revised rule: the always list is the frequency criterion
    \emph{unioned} with every source dataset's always list.

    \item \textbf{Fixed ANN re-prediction interval.} A constant
    $\Delta t_{\text{norm}}=0.2$~ms provided only a single mechanism update
    before ignition at high temperature ($\tau_{\text{ign}}=0.23$~ms at
    1500~K/1~atm). Revised rule: $\Delta t_{\text{norm}} = \min(0.2\,\text{ms},
    \tau_{\text{ign}}/50)$ and $\Delta t_{\text{ign}} = \min(\tau_{\text{ign}}/200,
    \Delta t_{\text{norm}}/4)$, guaranteeing $\geq 50$ mechanism updates during
    induction at every condition.

    \item \textbf{Ensemble selection by training loss.} The parallel training
    ensemble selected its best member by final \emph{training} loss, producing
    large run-to-run variance in sparse regimes. Revised: selection by
    \emph{validation} loss (12 seeds, early stopping with best-weight
    restoration).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Balanced Training Data and Never-List Audit}

Training data was assembled from four generation batches (baseline 15 cases;
NTC-stratified 25 cases, 750--1600~K; high-$T$ 30 cases, 1200--1600~K; mid-$T$
15 cases, 950--1150~K), pooled and \emph{temperature-balanced} by capping 500
samples per initial-temperature bin (bin edges 800/950/1100/1250/1400~K),
yielding 3000 training samples with partitions of 63 variable, 14 always, and
84 never species computed over the full pool.

An automated \emph{never-list audit} now precedes every training run: for each
validation condition, the union of classic-GPS species over the detailed
trajectory is checked against the never list; where blocked species exist, the
\emph{ceiling mechanism} (always $\cup$ variable species) must reproduce the
detailed ignition delay within 5\%, guaranteeing that no trained ANN is
structurally prevented from igniting correctly.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

The idt-scaled timestep rule uses the detailed ignition delay, available here
because validation runs the detailed simulation anyway; production use requires
a cheap ignition-delay estimate or a state-responsive interval controller. The
ANN input (T, P, 14 species) is an information bottleneck at the fresh-mixture
state; a hybrid scheme that evaluates classic GPS at the first interval (and
optionally at the heat-release-rate transition) and uses the ANN elsewhere
would eliminate the residual first-interval prediction error (T1500\_P1,
7.9\%) at negligible cost.

\subsection{Expanded Validation Campaign (in progress)}
